# Literary Agents for *The Seven AIs in Your Head*

A targeted shortlist of agents who have either represented the book's exact comparable titles (Mollick, Christian, Crawford), have deep histories in popular science/tech nonfiction, or have publicly stated they want serious AI/technology books. Every submission method below was verified against the agency's own website on the `last_verified` date. Where a method could not be verified, the agent has been moved to Tier 3 or excluded entirely.

- **Recent comparable deals:** Carried his nonfiction list from UTA to Calligraph in 2025; represents "influential and bestselling journalists, historians, science writers, and experts," with client work appearing in the *Wall Street Journal*, *New York Times*, *Wired*, *Bloomberg*, *LA Times*, *Forbes*, and *Fortune*. Long history at WME and Fletcher & Co before founding Calligraph. Specific recent deal titles are not posted publicly on his agency page, but he is a Publishers Marketplace dealmaker with a steady run of science and idea-driven nonfiction sales.
- **Submission:** Email `submissions@calligraphlit.com`. Query letter + brief synopsis + short bio + 25 pages of sample/proposal in the body of the email (no attachments). Subject line must include agent name, project title, and genre.
- **Known for:** Building careers for expert-driven nonfiction authors (journalists, scientists, historians) and moving them across the big-five houses. The Calligraph launch in 2025 was a deliberate boutique re-set after a decade and a half at WME and seven years at Fletcher.
- **Why this book fits:** He explicitly lists "science writers" and "experts across numerous fields" as the spine of his list. *The Seven AIs in Your Head* is exactly the science-writer-meets-original-data positioning he buys. Founder-of-new-agency status also means he is actively building, not closed.

## Query strategy

**Wave 1 (week 0).** Send simultaneously to five Tier 1 agents whose submission processes are independent: Eric Lupfer (Calligraph), Howard Yoon (WME), Daniel Greenberg (LGR), David Patterson (SKLA), and PJ Mark (Janklow). These are all email-or-form submissions with no cross-agency conflict. Use the same proposal package; tune the first paragraph of each query letter to that agent's specific comp deals (Lupfer: science-writer experts; Yoon: tech-meets-narrative; Greenberg: pop-psych-meets-tech; Patterson: idea-driven journalism; Mark: pop-culture narrative).

**Wave 2 (week 2).** Send to UTA — pick **one** of Pilar Queen *or* Christy Fletcher. The recommendation is Pilar Queen first, given she sold *Co-Intelligence*. Lead the query with the explicit differentiation from Mollick's book: *Co-Intelligence* teaches humans to use AI; *Seven AIs* diagnoses what the AIs actually are. If Queen passes, hold UTA — the agency's one-agent-only rule means a Fletcher follow-up is not available.

**Wave 3 (week 4, if needed).** Tier 2 agents: Adam Eaglin (Cheney), Toby Mundy (Aevitas UK — confirm he is in the drop-down first), Rafe Sagalyn (ICM/CAA), and Larry Weissman as a warm cold email. Reserve Mollie Glick and Jim Levine for the case where a referral can be secured first.

**Wave 4 (week 8+).** Tier 3 outreach only via warm introductions. The single highest-value introduction to pursue in parallel with all of the above is a path to **Margo Beth Fleming at Brockman** — through Brian Christian himself, through an Edge.org associate, or through any academic with a Brockman-repped book. If a referral lands, every other agent on this list defers.

**Response handling.** Assume "no response means no" for WME, Janklow, and Calligraph after 8 weeks. LGR and Aevitas have stated 4–6 week windows; respect those before nudging. UTA's form does not publish a response window; allow 8–10 weeks. Do not nudge more than once per agent.

**Posture for the queries themselves.** This book has an unusual structural advantage: the proposal contains *original empirical data* (the personality inventories actually run, the drift charts, the comparative figures). Most popular-AI books are synthesis. Lead every query with one sentence that makes the data point clear, then one sentence on the cultural stakes, then the comp triangle (Mollick / Christian / Crawford). The author's EarthPilot.ai founder credential and prior shipping history should go in the bio paragraph, not the hook.
